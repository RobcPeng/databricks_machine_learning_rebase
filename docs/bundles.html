The bundles & their data

Two bundles, two data domains, one catalog.

Both bundles write to the rpeng_upleveling catalog, one schema family each: computer vision over sports video on one side, four public education and labor datasets on the other. Below is what each one models, the example data it runs on, the medallion flow that feeds it, and the serverless infrastructure it stands up.

obj_recg · bundle uplevels_cv

Seeing the game

Computer vision on serverless GPU. The motivating task, in the bundle's own words: locate the athlete and equipment with bounding boxes, then produce the pose keypoints of a golf swing, the keypoints that connect into the skeleton overlay you see drawn on a swing video.

Layer 1 · computer vision

Detect & trace

Three vision tasks over sports video frames:

  • Object detection: bounding boxes around athlete and equipment (golfer, club, ball).
  • 2D pose: the 17 COCO person keypoints, connected into the swing skeleton.
  • 3D pose: 33 BlazePose landmarks with depth, for swing-plane and rotation analysis that 2D cannot reach.
Layer 2 · team performance

Predict from what was seen

CV-derived features (biomechanics angles, bounding-box areas, visible keypoints) plus game-result CSVs feed tabular XGBoost models across seven targets:

win probability · points scored · points allowed · offensive efficiency · defensive efficiency · per-athlete performance index · scholarship ROI.

Parameterized by sport

Golf is the default and the primary motivation, but the same bundle runs on football, basketball, baseball, hockey, and tennis without code changes. The sport is a job parameter (sport=golf) and a one-entry registry. Detection classes, signature events, and biomechanics angles all live in a per-sport spec.

Vision models

Six families, nine specs. Three axes decide which one you pick. Detection or pose: boxes around objects, or keypoints on a body. One-stage or two-stage: predict boxes in a single pass, or propose regions first and classify them second, trading speed against precision. 2D or 3D: pixel coordinates, or coordinates with depth.

Detectors, to find the athlete and the equipment

Detection specs · task = detect
SpecArchitectureTypeReach for it when
yolo_detect YOLO11-nano (Ultralytics) One-stage, anchor-free You want the fastest train and inference and a strong real-time baseline. The nano variant is the smallest, lowest-latency option in the zoo.
cnn_detect SSDlite · MobileNetV3-Large One-stage, lightweight You are targeting mobile or edge. Smallest parameter count and fastest inference of the torchvision family, the accuracy-to-speed lower bound.
resnet_detect Faster R-CNN · ResNet-50 FPN v2 Two-stage Accuracy matters more than latency. The established COCO benchmark; the head resizes to sport-specific classes.
fast_rcnn_detect Faster R-CNN fed external Selective Search proposals, RPN bypassed Two-stage, classical proposals You want the historical ablation: the generation-1 approach of classical region proposals instead of a learned RPN. Useful for teaching and comparison.
rtdetr_detect RT-DETR (large) Transformer You want attention-based, anchor-free accuracy and can spend more than YOLO-nano. It also supplies the person boxes for the top-down pose model.

Pose models, to trace the swing

Pose specs · task = pose / pose3d
SpecArchitectureOutputReach for it when
yolo_pose YOLO11-nano pose 17 COCO keypoints, one pass You want end-to-end pose with no separate detector and the same latency edge as YOLO detection.
resnet_pose Keypoint R-CNN · ResNet-50 FPN 17 COCO keypoints You want two-stage keypoints with strong localization from the region proposals, and speed is not the constraint.
rtmpose_pose RTMPose-m (MMPose), top-down 17 COCO keypoints You want the highest 2D keypoint accuracy and can run a detector first to supply person boxes (it pairs with rtdetr_detect).
blazepose_pose3d MediaPipe BlazePose (full) 33 landmarks with depth (x, y, z) You need 3D, for swing-plane and rotation. Runs on any CPU, no training, instant results. Pretrained and inference-only.
One-stage vs. two-stage

One-stage detectors (YOLO, SSDlite) predict boxes directly: fast, deployable, a touch less precise on small or overlapping objects. Two-stage detectors (Faster and Keypoint R-CNN) propose regions and then classify them: slower, but stronger localization. The benchmark exists to put a number on that trade for your footage.

Top-down pose has a dependency

RTMPose is top-down: it needs person boxes before it can place keypoints. That is a pipeline cost, not a flaw, and it buys accuracy. BlazePose is the opposite bargain: no detector, no GPU, no training, but you trade some 2D precision for depth the others cannot produce.

How they are scored

Every model reports on the same footing: bounding-box mAP, keypoint OKS, p50 and p95 latency, parameter count, and on-disk size, delegating to the same COCO scorer the public leaderboards use. The 3D model is down-projected to the 17 COCO keypoints for a comparable 2D score; its true 3D error metric waits on 3D ground truth. See the roadmap for which evaluators are live.

The example data

Annotations are standard COCO JSON: person_keypoints_<split>.json for keypoints and instances_<split>.json for boxes, across train, val, and test. Game results are per-sport CSVs (game_id, team, opponent, game_date, points_for, points_against, off_efficiency, def_efficiency).

Bring your own frames

The data is not shipped with the bundle. The volume layout is created empty and operators stage labeled data into it; the setup notebook documents the exact file paths expected per sport. Nothing about the benchmark fabricates footage or labels.

Medallion flow

A serverless Lakeflow Declarative Pipeline moves data through four schemas whose numbered names sort in medallion order. Streaming Auto Loader lands raw files, bronze explodes the COCO arrays into typed rows, silver drops degenerate records, and gold assembles the training manifest and the leaderboard.

00

Landing · seeing_models_00_landing

Auto Loader ingests raw label JSON, frame image metadata, and game CSVs, one row per file.

01

Bronze · seeing_models_01_bronze

Explodes COCO arrays into images, keypoints (bbox, keypoints, visible count), and games (with a derived win). No filtering yet.

02

Silver · seeing_models_02_silver

Quality expectations drop records: unknown splits, zero-area boxes, instances with fewer than five visible keypoints, negative scores.

03

Gold · seeing_models_03_gold

training_manifest, athlete_metrics, and leakage-safe team_game_features as views; model_comparison (the CV leaderboard) and team_predictions written by the training code.

Infrastructure

Compute
Serverless GPU, default type A10. Training dispatches through a distributed wrapper, with a classic T4 or A100 fallback and an in-process path for local runs.
Pipeline
Serverless Lakeflow Declarative Pipeline with Photon; source in transformations/.
Job
train_and_compare: one task per model family, each on its own environment (cv_env, mmpose_env, mediapipe_env, teamperf_env); parameter sport (default golf).
MLflow
One experiment per family. Each successful model registers to Unity Catalog and moves its @prod alias to the new version.
Catalog
rpeng_upleveling (must pre-exist); four bundle-managed schemas plus a managed cv_data volume.

pca_ica · bundle dr_bench

Reading the outcome

Scikit-learn on serverless CPU, and fully runnable off-platform for fast local iteration. The core question the bundle asks: given what we know about a graduate, an institution, or a student, can we predict an above-median earnings or attainment outcome, and do PCA, ICA, random projection, or clustering-derived features help the classic models get there?

Balanced by construction

Every target is a median split of a continuous outcome, so label = 1 means above median. That makes the classes a clean 50/50, so accuracy, F1, and ROC-AUC are all honest, and ROC-AUC is the primary ranking column.

Four public datasets across the education-to-labor pipeline

All four are US-anchored and public. Together they span the grains a SLED or higher-ed analyst actually works at: the individual graduate, the institution, the working adult, and the enrolled student.

Known gap

The College Scorecard source currently fails to land. Serverless egress reaches the NSF, Census, and OECD hosts but not the Scorecard download host. The spec, target, and features are already validated against the real file; only the fetch path needs to change. The bronze step skips a failed source and continues, so the other three still run. This is tracked on the roadmap.

What the features look like

Each dataset carries a curated set of numeric and categorical columns: the levers a program can actually pull, alongside the demographics it must account for.

nscg_2023 · graduate

Numeric: age, weekly hours, weeks worked.

Categorical: degree type, bachelor's major field, school Carnegie classification, employer sector and size, and four job-satisfaction scales, alongside sex, race, Hispanic origin, marital status, and citizenship.

scorecard_2026 · institution

Numeric: admission rate, SAT average, ACT midpoint, enrollment, average cost, in-state and out-of-state tuition, Pell share, 4-year completion, enrollment shares by race.

Categorical: control (public, nonprofit, for-profit), region, urbanicity, predominant and highest degree, state.

acs_co_2024 · working adult

Numeric: age, usual weekly hours.

Categorical: educational attainment, field of degree, occupation, class of worker, plus sex, race, marital status, citizenship, and Hispanic origin.

pisa_2022 · student

Numeric: economic, social and cultural status, home possessions, parental education and occupation, math, reading and science proficiency, sense of belonging, growth mindset, work mastery.

Categorical: gender, immigrant status, grade, grade repetition, country.

Three schemas, five notebook tasks. Data lands verbatim, gets filtered and labeled in silver, then the grid sweep and leaderboard run over the curated tables. Off-platform, the same code fetches from the public URLs instead of reading silver.
