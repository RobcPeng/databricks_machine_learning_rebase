Dimensionality reduction

From a thousand columns to the few factors that move an outcome.

SLED and higher-ed data is wide. A graduate survey carries hundreds of fields; a one-hot encoding of majors, occupations, and institutions pushes that into the thousands. Dimensionality reduction asks a sharp question: how many independent factors actually carry the signal, and once you name them, which institutional and student actions do they correspond to?

The problem

Width hides the signal

After the shared preprocessor imputes, scales, and one-hot-encodes, a dataset like the College Scorecard or the graduate survey can hand a classifier a very wide feature matrix. Most of that width is redundant: correlated columns, rare categories, noise. Reduction compresses it, and the way it compresses is itself informative.

The pipeline puts reduction in a fixed position, so the reducer is often the only thing that changes between two runs, which is what makes its effect measurable.

A

Preprocess

Impute and scale numeric columns; one-hot encode categoricals, capped at 50 categories to bound width.

B

Reduce

Optional: PCA, ICA, or random projection to k components (default 10). Or none, the control.

C

Cluster features

Optional: append one-hot cluster membership from KMeans or a Gaussian mixture.

D

Model

Any classifier from the registry, scored by cross-validated ROC-AUC.

The four reducers

Three ways to compress, and a control

none · the control

No reduction

The full preprocessed feature space goes straight to the classifier. Every other reducer is judged against this line. If PCA at 10 components matches none, ten factors carried what hundreds of columns did.

pca · variance

Principal Component Analysis

Rotate the data to find the directions of greatest spread, keep the top k. Each component is a weighted blend of every original feature, and the components are uncorrelated. It reports how much variance each one retains, logged as pca_explained_var.

Trade: assumes linear structure; components mix many features, so reading them takes work. Fast and principled.

ica · independence

Independent Component Analysis

Go past uncorrelated to statistically independent. FastICA finds axes that are as mutually unrelated as possible by maximizing non-Gaussianity, the classic recipe for separating mixed signals into their sources.

Trade: components are unordered and can fail to converge; costlier than PCA. Worth it when the true drivers are independent and non-Gaussian.

random_projection · speed

Gaussian Random Projection

Multiply the features by a random matrix. The Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma guarantees pairwise distances survive, roughly, without ever looking at the data, so it is nearly free.

Trade: captures no semantic structure; adds draw-to-draw noise. A fast, data-agnostic baseline and a sanity check against PCA and ICA.

Clustering as features

Adding "which cohort is this?" as a signal

After reduction, the harness can fit a clusterer on the reduced space and append one-hot cluster membership to the feature matrix. It answers a different question than the classifier: not "what is the outcome" but "which region of the population does this record fall in," a structural hint that can lift boundary-based models.

kmeans · 4 clusters

Partitions the reduced space into four groups by minimizing within-cluster spread. Each record gets a four-column one-hot flag. In SLED terms: a data-driven segmentation of graduates, institutions, or students into cohorts.

gmm · registered, off by default

A Gaussian mixture allows clusters of different sizes and shapes rather than spheres. Available in the registry; add it to the sweep config to compare against KMeans.

The model step · seven registered, six in the sweep

Classic classifiers

The last stage of the pipeline is the classifier itself. The sweep runs each dataset through a family of tabular models, from the interpretable linear baseline to gradient-boosted trees. The goal is not to crown one. It is to see how each responds to the reducer in front of it, which is where the interesting differences show up.

Reading SLED outcomes from the factors

How reduction points at the actions that matter

The datasets mix two kinds of columns: attributes a program cannot change (age, birthplace, prior demographics) and actions it can (admissions selectivity, price, aid, advising, program design, the choice of field, hours worked, school climate). Reduction is how you find which bundles of those actions co-move with an above-median outcome.

A component is a hypothesis

Each PCA component is a weighted recipe over the features. Read its heaviest loadings and it names a latent factor. A component loading on low admit rate, high SAT, high cost, and high completion is a "selectivity and resources" factor; one loading on Pell share, open admission, and regional locale is an "access mission" factor. When such a factor aligns with the outcome direction, it tells you which levers to look at first.

ICA separates tangled drivers

Family resources and institutional investment often blur together in raw survey columns. Because ICA pulls apart independent signals, it can isolate a "student background" source from a "program intensity" source, so you can ask which one is actually associated with the outcome, rather than crediting a mixture of both.

A discipline, not a shortcut to causation

Reduction surfaces associations: which actions co-move with an outcome and how much predictive signal they carry. That is where to point a causal study, not a substitute for one. The harness measures predictive lift honestly (balanced classes, cross-validated ROC-AUC); turning a strong factor into a claimed intervention is the analyst's next step, with the appropriate method.

Reading the benchmark

Three questions the leaderboard answers

Q1

Does reduction beat none?

For each model, compare PCA, ICA, and random projection against the full feature space. If a reducer wins, a compact set of factors carries the signal better than the raw width.

Q2

Do cluster features add lift?

Compare clustering = kmeans against none for the same model and reducer. When cohort membership helps, population structure is doing predictive work.

Q3

How does explained variance track accuracy?

Watch pca_explained_var against ROC-AUC across component counts. It tells you how few factors you can keep before prediction degrades.

Each grid cell reports cross-validated and held-out accuracy, F1, and ROC-AUC; ROC-AUC is the primary ranking column, and every cell is logged as a nested MLflow run. The default sweep is 192 cells: four datasets, four reducer settings, two clustering settings, six models. No results are published on this site; the harness produces them when the job runs.
